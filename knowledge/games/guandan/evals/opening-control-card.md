# Opening control-card opportunity cost

## Regression that motivated this case

On the first lead with 27 cards, the coach recommended a ten-to-ace straight
flush solely to preserve a four-card bomb. The explanation valued immediate card
shedding without comparing loss of future control or partnership tempo.

## What this eval asserts

This is a critique/ranking eval, not a rule that forbids opening a straight flush.
A passing analysis must:

1. retain the straight flush among legal candidates;
2. compare its future control value with at least one lower-cost lead;
3. discuss partner impact and the absence of observed history;
4. avoid claiming knowledge of hidden cards or invented win rates;
5. recommend it only when it gives a concrete whole-deal advantage.

## Counterexamples

Opening the straight flush may be reasonable when it creates a credible immediate
finish, repairs an otherwise much worse hand structure, supports a known partner
plan from prior public actions, or answers a late-stage opponent threat. Those
conditions were absent in the reported first-lead position.

Do not promote this note to `verified` until the position is reconstructed with
